    01

    What we're proposing

    A community theater is a company made of the people who live in a place. Not touring professionals — teachers, students, retirees, shift workers, and people who have never been on a stage and would like to try.

    We intend to establish one in Portales: a standing organisation with a board, a budget, and policies, capable of producing work year after year. The first production is planned for spring 2027. It is the proof of concept, not the purpose.

    A theater that closes after opening night isn't a theater. It's an event. We are building the organisation first, and the show is how we prove it works.

    We are early. The organisation is not yet incorporated, no venue is confirmed, no production has been chosen, and no funding has been raised. We are talking to potential partners now because the decisions that matter most are the ones made before any of that is fixed.

    02

    Why Portales

    Roosevelt County has roughly 19,200 residents, about 12,100 of them in Portales. It has a university with a theatre programme and a purpose-built performance facility. It has a civic culture that already turns out for the county fair and the Peanut Valley Festival.

    What we understand it does not have is a theater company outside the university that any resident can simply join. Students graduate and leave. Faculty productions serve the academic calendar. Between them sits a large group of people — adults with jobs, families new to the area, retirees, teenagers not enrolled at ENMU — with no route onto a stage.

    A community theater is unusually good value for a small town. It needs no building of its own, employs volunteers, draws audiences from a wide rural catchment, and gives people a reason to spend an evening in town rather than driving to Clovis or Lubbock. It also gives young people a place to be after school that is neither sport nor screen.

    03

    The founding production

    Spring 2027. The show has not been chosen — deliberately. We are confirming a venue and dates first, because those constraints determine what can realistically be staged, and choosing a title before knowing the room is how amateur companies get into trouble.

    Selection will run through a scorecard weighing cast size, technical demands, rights availability and cost, volunteer capacity, audience appeal, and how well the piece suits this community. We will carry genuine second and third choices, because performance rights can be refused.

    Our working assumption is three performances. Everything else — budget, ticket pricing, marketing reach — follows from the venue decision.

    How it gets paid for

    We researched this before asking anyone for anything, and the finding shaped the plan.

    State and federal arts grants cannot fund this production. New Mexico Arts requires tax-exempt status and approves awards in July, paying by reimbursement — money would arrive months after opening night. The state tourism programmes run on the same July fiscal year. The National Endowment for the Arts requires years of prior programming and does not accept fiscally sponsored applicants.

    So a founding production has to be funded locally. We are planning on that basis rather than hoping a grant lands.

    09

    Beyond the first show

    The founding production is designed to leave things behind: policies and templates, a volunteer roster, an audience list, sponsor and vendor relationships, an equipment and costume inventory, a budget history, and a written record of what worked.

    Year two stabilises operations, keeps the supporters gained in year one, and adds programming. Year three establishes a repeatable season, more diversified funding, stronger governance, and a plan for leadership succession so the organisation does not depend on whoever happened to start it.

    These are planning scenarios rather than promises. We would rather say clearly that we intend to still exist in 2030 than quietly hope for it.

    The next step

    We would welcome a conversation. There is no decision to make today — we are looking for guidance on whether this is worth pursuing, and what the right process would be.
