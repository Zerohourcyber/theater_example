What we're asking of ENMU

  1. A conversation about venue availability for a community production in spring 2027, and what dates could realistically work around the department's own season.
  2. An understanding of the requirements — insurance minimums, technical staffing, deposits, box office, custodial, and how far in advance a request needs to be made.
  3. Guidance on the right process and the right office. We would rather follow the university's procedure correctly than ask for an exception.
  4. Whether a partnership interests you at all — and if so, in what form. We are not assuming one.

What we think ENMU might get from it: a standing local audience for the theater program, a place for students to work alongside community members, a reason for alumni and residents to be on campus, and a partner organization that can eventually carry its own costs.

We are not asking for money, and we are not asking for a decision today.

What we already know about the constraints

Some of this we researched before asking, so we would not waste your time.

The venue is the whole schedule

The University Theatre Center hosts around eight productions a year. A spring slot may already be committed to the department's own season, and we understand that comes first.

That is why this is the first conversation we're having, before choosing a show or announcing a date. If April doesn't work, we would rather hear it now and plan around it than build a season on an assumption.

Grant money won't arrive in time

New Mexico Arts notifies awards in July and pays on reimbursement. The state tourism programs run on the July fiscal year. The NEA requires years of programming history and won't accept a fiscally sponsored applicant.

So a first production has to be funded locally — sponsorships, individual gifts, in-kind support, ticket revenue, and possibly city lodgers' tax for marketing. We're planning on that basis rather than hoping a grant lands.
